# Agent 2: What Goes Wrong (Clause Types)

## Method
Used WebSearch only (8 searches, 0 page fetches beyond search-result summaries - search tool returned sufficient extracted detail from primary sources so no separate WebFetch calls were needed). Searches covered: CFPB complaint database + arbitration, FTC negative option/auto-renewal, freelancer union survey on late payment/kill fees/IP, security deposit tenant disputes, FTC non-compete rule prevalence, ToS class-action waiver studies, gig platform unilateral deactivation, and small-business litigation clause surveys. Did not search: fee escalators/late fee junk-fee data, personal guarantees, subletting bans, confidentiality overreach, jurisdiction/venue, unilateral amendment ("we may change these terms") as a standalone topic (folded into ToS/arbitration results), net-90 payment terms specifically. Stayed under the 12-search / 15-page caps; stopped once 8 sourced findings were reached.

## Findings

1. **Freelance nonpayment is the single best-quantified freelance pain point.** Freelancers Union's widely-cited 2014 survey found 34% of freelancers experienced nonpayment in the prior year, with average wait times of 90+ days; a more recent (undated, secondary-sourced) figure claims 59% are owed $50,000+ in unpaid invoices. Contracts materially help: freelancers with written contracts report 73% fewer payment disputes than those without. Source: https://www.plutio.com/freelancer-magazine/how-to-create-a-freelance-contract , https://docually.com/freelance-contracts/freelance-payment-protection/

2. **Auto-renewal/negative-option complaints are large in raw volume and are the FTC's current enforcement priority.** The FTC's Sentinel complaint system logged 100,000+ negative-option complaints over five years (tens of thousands per year), concentrated in failure to disclose the negative option, enrollment without consent, and burdensome cancellation. Named recent enforcement targets: Amazon Prime, Uber, LA Fitness. Source: https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions

3. **Arbitration + class-action waivers rarely generate individual complaints but systematically block collective redress.** CFPB's landmark study (analysis of ~850 contracts, 1,800 arbitration filings, 3,400 federal lawsuits) found companies invoke arbitration clauses to kill class actions 65% of the time once sued, and that arbitration clauses cover >80 million credit-card holders and banks representing 44% of insured deposits. Three in four consumers don't even know if their agreement has one. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-finds-that-arbitration-agreements-limit-relief-for-consumers/

4. **Security deposit disputes are consistently ranked the #1 or top landlord-tenant dispute category** by multiple property-management and legal sources, though no single authoritative national tally (e.g., a state AG or HUD complaint count) was located in this search pass - the "#1" ranking rests on repeated industry citation (Rental Protection Agency trend report referenced secondhand) rather than a primary complaint database. Source: https://american-apartment-owners-association.org/property-management/latest-news/10-most-common-apartment-complaints/

5. **Non-competes affect a very large share of the workforce but with weak negotiating power.** FTC's 2024 rulemaking record: ~1 in 5 workers (~30 million, ~18%) bound by non-competes; under 10% of workers can negotiate them; 93% sign despite reading them; 30-40% are asked to sign only after already accepting the job offer (i.e., after losing bargaining leverage). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/features/noncompetes , https://www.nelp.org/on-the-ftcs-non-compete-contracts-rule/

6. **Debt-collection-related complaints in the CFPB database frequently hinge on a "failure to validate" issue** - 28% of complaints against certain debt collectors were about the collector never providing enough information to verify the debt exists/amount, which is a contract-adjacent documentation failure rather than a single named clause type. Source: https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CFPB-Complaints-Fact-Sheet-4-1.pdf

7. **Small-business contract litigation is most often attributed by law-firm sources to indemnification, non-compete, and termination provisions**, with indemnity clauses singled out as capable of transferring catastrophic, open-ended liability ("any and all claims") onto a small business. This is practitioner commentary, not a court-docket statistical study. Source: https://www.aikenslawfirm.com/the-contract-clauses-that-create-the-most-litigation-for-small-businesses/

8. **Gig-platform account deactivation (a unilateral-termination variant) is described as high-frequency and high-impact** by Human Rights Watch's 2025 report on US platform work and by WageIndicator's worker-story collection, citing automated/algorithmic deactivation with little transparency or appeal - but neither source gives a numeric complaint count or rate. Source: https://www.hrw.org/report/2025/05/12/the-gig-trap/algorithmic-wage-and-labor-exploitation-in-platform-work-in-the-us

## What I could not find

- **No frequency/complaint-count data at all** for: fee escalators/late-fee clauses as a standalone category (only found under the general "junk fees" FTC umbrella, not searched directly), personal guarantees, subletting bans, exclusivity clauses, confidentiality/NDA overreach, jurisdiction/venue clauses, or "unilateral amendment" ("we may change these terms") as its own tracked category - these were folded into or overshadowed by the ToS/arbitration search results but never independently quantified.
- **No single authoritative national database count** for security deposit disputes (e.g., a HUD or state attorney general tabulation) - ranking rests on repeated industry-source claims of "#1 dispute type," not a primary count.
- **No litigation-frequency dataset** (e.g., court docket analysis) for indemnification/limitation-of-liability clauses - only law-firm commentary asserting these are common litigation triggers.
- **No quantified complaint rate** for gig-platform unilateral deactivation - qualitative/advocacy sourcing only (HRW, WageIndicator), no numeric database like CFPB's or FTC's.
- The freelance nonpayment "34%" figure is from a 2014 study; could not confirm a more recent, primary-sourced (non-secondary-blog) update, though blog sources claim newer higher figures ($50k+ owed to 59% of freelancers) without a locatable primary study link.
- Did not verify IP-assignment/work-for-hire clause frequency against any complaint or litigation database - only qualitative "common mistake" framing from freelancer-advice blogs, not from a legal-aid or bar-association source.
