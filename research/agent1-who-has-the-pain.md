# Who Has The Pain

## 1. Disney+ terms of service used to try to block a wrongful death lawsuit
> "You and Disney agree to resolve, by binding individual arbitration as provided below, all Disputes (including any related disputes involving The Walt Disney Company, its subsidiaries, or its affiliates)"

Source: https://www.jnd-law.com/disneys-clause-and-effect-arbitration-in-the-magic-kingdom/

Context: Jeffrey Piccolo signed up for a Disney+ free trial in 2019, agreeing to its terms of service without reading the arbitration clause. After his wife died of anaphylaxis at a Disney World restaurant in 2023, Disney initially argued the wrongful-death claim had to go to private arbitration instead of a jury trial - because of that 2019 Disney+ signup, unrelated to the restaurant visit. Clause type: mandatory arbitration / class-action waiver buried in consumer ToS.

## 2. Piccolo's lawyer on the scope of the arbitration clause
> "anyone who signs up for a Disney+ account, even for a short-lived free trial, irrevocably waives their right to a jury trial"

Source: https://www.jnd-law.com/disneys-clause-and-effect-arbitration-in-the-magic-kingdom/

Context: Plaintiff's counsel describing how broadly Disney tried to apply a clause the signer had no realistic way of noticing mattered years later. Clause type: arbitration clause / dispute resolution.

## 3. Freelance/creator non-compete clauses causing people to turn down work out of fear
> "I've had a number of young creators come to me over the years who were so intimidated by this clause in their contracts that the creators had turned down work repeatedly for fear of violating this agreement."

Source: https://colleendoran.substack.com/p/the-none-compete-clause

Context: Comic artist and industry veteran Colleen Doran describing young freelance creators who signed non-compete clauses in work contracts without understanding their reach, then lost income by refusing other jobs out of fear of breaching them. Clause type: non-compete.

## 4. Ren faire/seasonal performer non-compete buried mid-contract
> "forbade the signer from working for any other ren faire or Halloween event within 100 miles for a full year"

Source: https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/ (aggregating an AskReddit thread of contract fine-print stories)

Context: A performer signed an acting/event contract and only later discovered a broad geographic and time-based non-compete restricting future seasonal work. Clause type: non-compete.

## 5. Employer non-compete with near-unlimited scope
> "geographically encompassed the entire globe as well as any profession even closely related"

Source: https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/

Context: An employee described an employment contract non-compete clause with effectively worldwide geographic scope and an overly broad definition of competing work, discovered only after the fact. Clause type: non-compete.

## 6. Funeral home "small fee" clause worth 18% of a payout
> "the 'small fee' is 18% of the total life insurance claim!"

Source: https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/

Context: A person signing a funeral services contract did not realize a vaguely worded "small fee" for filing insurance paperwork was actually a percentage-based fee amounting to roughly $180,000 on a $1 million claim. Clause type: hidden/ambiguous fee clause.

## 7. Car dealership data-sharing clause buried in paperwork
> "by signing the paperwork it gave them permission to 'lend' my information to third parties"

Source: https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/

Context: A car buyer signed dealership paperwork without noticing a clause authorizing the dealer to share/sell their personal information to third parties. Clause type: data-sharing/consent clause.

## 8. Mural/commissioned-art contract clause blocking the right to sue
> "worded to prevent me from suing them if they broke contract"

Source: https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/

Context: An artist doing commissioned work (a mural, described as an "exposure" gig) signed a contract that included language waiving their ability to sue if the other party breached the agreement, discovered only after a dispute arose. Clause type: liability waiver / right-to-sue waiver.
